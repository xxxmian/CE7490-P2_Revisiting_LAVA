Mechanism
LAVA assumes hosts represent lifetime cohorts. A low-confidence Best-Fit decision can inject an incompatible VM; later high-confidence decisions cannot undo that history.
New hypothesis
Keep confidence decisions, but isolate their effects: once a host enters baseline or lifetime-aware mode, retain that mode until the host becomes empty.
The redesign follows measured failure evidence—not a search for favorable parameters.

02
The problem: packing today can fragment tomorrow
Multidimensional fragmentation
CPU and memory can remain individually free but unusable together.
Time is the hidden dimension
VMs that fit equally well may exit hours—or months—apart.
Operational consequence
Fewer empty hosts means lower obtainability, less maintenance flexibility, and weaker energy opportunities.
Research objective: maximize time-weighted empty-host percentage without rejecting VMs.

03
What LAVA adds to conventional bin packing
1 · Distributional prediction
Predict remaining-lifetime distributions rather than a single permanent label.
2 · Re-prediction
Condition on uptime so initially unpredictable long-lived VMs can be corrected later.
3 · Host lifecycle state
Hosts move from empty → open → recycling and adapt their lifetime class.
4 · Temporal placement
Prefer placements that allow host cohorts to exit together.
